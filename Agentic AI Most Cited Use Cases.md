1. Customer support resolution
The single most recycled enterprise example. Agent reads a ticket, looks up CRM/order/policy data, refunds or resets, drafts or sends the reply, escalates only exceptions. Klarna, Intercom Fin, Zendesk, Gartner’s “80% of common issues by 2029” line, and almost every use-case roundup lead with this.

2. Software engineering agents
Write code, open PRs, fix bugs, generate tests, modernize legacy code. In developer discourse and some deployment telemetry this is the dominant agent use; one cited snapshot put coding near half of agent tool calls. Cursor, Claude Code, GitHub Copilot Agent, Devin, Jules are the usual names.

3. IT service desk and incident response
Internal Tier-1 tickets, password/access resets, runbook execution, alert triage, MTTR reduction. ServiceNow-style ITSM agents and “DevOps agent correlates logs + recent commits” stories appear constantly. IBM AskHR is adjacent and equally over-cited. 

4. Sales development and lead qualification
Research a prospect, personalize outreach, run sequences, qualify inbound, book meetings, keep the CRM current. SDR agents (11x, Artisan, Salesforce Agentforce SDR) are a staple of B2B content. 

5. Fraud, AML, and real-time risk
Watch transactions, score anomalies, draft SARs, cut false positives. Banks and payments vendors repeat this more than almost any other finance use case. Stripe Radar / bank investigation agents are the stock examples. 

6. Supply chain, inventory, and logistics rerouting
Detect a disruption, find alternate suppliers or routes, reallocate stock, notify customers. Present in nearly every industry-agnostic list (McKinsey, TechTarget, Red Hat, KDnuggets). 

7. HR service desk, recruiting, and onboarding
Policy Q&A, resume screening, interview scheduling, access provisioning, new-hire checklists. IBM AskHR (often quoted at ~94% containment) is one of the most copied case studies on the internet. 

8. Finance operations: invoices, AP, reconciliation, close
Match invoice to PO, code the spend, flag exceptions, draft the close pack. High volume, structured data, easy-to-cite time savings, so listicles never skip it. 

9. Security operations
Detect, enrich, isolate, write detection rules, hunt. CrowdStrike / Microsoft Security Copilot / “agentic SOC” coverage is dense because vendors and CISOs both talk about it. 

10. Enterprise knowledge and research assistants
Ask across internal docs, then take a next action (open a ticket, draft a brief, update a record). JPMorgan’s LLM Suite / advisor knowledge agents get cited endlessly. Overlaps with “agentic RAG.” 

11. Healthcare admin: documentation, coding, prior auth, claims
Draft notes, assign ICD/CPT, assemble payer packets, track status. Clinical diagnosis is discussed less than this admin stack, which is where the volume of articles sits. 

12. Marketing campaign creation and optimization
Generate variants, launch, watch performance, reallocate spend, personalize next-best offer. McKinsey growth-function pieces and vendor blogs treat this as a core agent domain. 

13. Personal computer-use agents: shop, book, fill forms, travel
Operator / ChatGPT Agent / Project Mariner-style “use the browser for me.” Less common on enterprise lists, extremely common in consumer media and product launches: groceries, reservations, flights, checkout. 

14. Procurement and supplier negotiation
Compare bids, monitor vendor risk, draft or negotiate terms, trigger reorders. Microsoft and McKinsey ops writing both push this hard in 2025–2026. 

15. Legal contract review and due diligence
Redline against a playbook, extract clauses, flag risk, assemble diligence. High mention count because every “agents for professional services” article needs it. 

16. Deep research and competitive intelligence
Multi-step web/document research, source synthesis, brief writing. OpenAI Deep Research and “competitive monitoring agents” are repeated in both consumer and enterprise coverage. 

17. Manufacturing quality, scheduling, and predictive maintenance
Spot defects, reconfigure a line, order parts, alert technicians. Slightly less ubiquitous than supply-chain logistics, but a fixture of industrial agent articles. 

18. Data-analyst agents
Text-to-SQL, dashboarding, anomaly explanation, self-serve analytics. Delivery Hero’s QueryAnswerBird, Snyk’s internal data agent, and “analytics will be most impacted by agents” survey results keep this high. Gartner has ranked analytics/BI among the domains leaders expect agents to hit hardest. 

19. Insurance claims and underwriting
Intake, document gathering, fraud flags, reserve suggestion, policy checks. Separate from generic “fraud” because insurance content treats claims as its own end-to-end agent workflow. 

20. Scientific / drug-discovery research loops
Literature review, candidate suggestion, protocol drafting, result analysis. More aspirational than production-heavy, but it is still one of the most repeated “future flagship” use cases in healthcare/pharma agent writing.
